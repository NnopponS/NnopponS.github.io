WheelSense & EaseAI

A wheelchair at home started the project. A room full of caregivers changed the problem.

WheelSense began as a team-built home prototype for wheelchair users. EaseAI takes the same sensing ideas into care facilities, where staff need to know what deserves their attention and what they can do about it.

02 / SYSTEM DESIGN

Following the signal.

A stream of motion readings only matters if it reaches the right interface with enough context. My work covered the path from the device to the application.

  1. 01 / SENSE

    Embedded firmware

    I contributed to wheelchair-device firmware and Bluetooth Low Energy integration for motion sensing and indoor localization.

  2. 02 / CONNECT

    Telemetry & backend

    I helped connect devices and apps to the backend. MQTT carries telemetry, while FastAPI and PostgreSQL make it available to the application layer.

  3. 03 / PRESENT

    Mobile & web apps

    The Flutter gateway pairs with the sensors and forwards data. The Next.js dashboard presents that information to different user roles.

03 / FROM HOME TO CARE FACILITIES

What EaseAI adds.

At home, the assistant could provide reminders, offer exercise suggestions and control appliances. For a nursing-home setting, the team explored how software could help staff prioritize tasks and respond to safety concerns for different residents.

EaseAI keeps a proposed action separate from execution so users can review it first. Its software architecture was registered with Thailand’s Department of Intellectual Property in May 2026. This is a software copyright, not a patent.

WheelSense and EaseAI are prototypes. The work does not establish clinical effectiveness or measured staffing outcomes.
